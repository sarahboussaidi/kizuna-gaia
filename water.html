Chapter 二  ·  Water

Water

Japanese water wisdom × Tunisian water heritage

One country organised abundance. The other organised scarcity. Both ended up with rules about who gets water and when — and the rules are the part that travels.

Chapter 2 of 7

Japan  日本

Water as a shared obligation

In Japan’s terraced valleys, mountain streams feed the fields through community-managed canals. Who waters when is settled by long-standing village custom rather than by whoever opens the gate first, and the canals are cleaned together every year.

Tunisia  تونس

Water as something you catch

Jessour and meskat do the opposite job: instead of sharing a reliable flow, they capture a rare one. Low dikes across a dry valley hold back the runoff of a few storms a year, with the silt it carries, and a crop grows in what builds up behind them.

What Gaia builds on it

Four water modules.

Traditional Techniques Matching

Demo

Describe a parcel and Gaia returns the traditional technique — from either country — that fits its slope, soil and rainfall.

Runs in the browser on a hand-written table of documented techniques. Try it in chapter 五.

Smart Irrigation AI

Planned

Recommends when and how much to water, from the weather forecast, soil type and crop stage.

Needs a trained model and a weather feed. Designed, not built.

Rainwater Intelligence

Planned

Estimates how much of a coming storm a parcel can actually hold, given its dikes and terraces.

Needs elevation data per parcel. Designed, not built.

Water Sharing

Planned

A published irrigation turn for neighbours on one source, with the right to hand your slot to someone who needs it more.

Borrowed from the Japanese village turn. Needs accounts and a shared schedule.

Working Demo Planned

Every feature carries one of these. Working runs for real; Demo works on sample values; Planned is designed but not built.
