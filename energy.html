Chapter 三  ·  Energy

Energy

Japanese green technology × Tunisian solar potential

Japan put panels above crops because it had no spare land. Tunisia has the sun. The question is not whether to copy the technique, but at what height.

Chapter 3 of 7

Japan  日本

Solar sharing

Japanese growers raise narrow solar panels on frames high above a field, spaced so part of the light still reaches the crop. The land keeps farming while it also makes electricity. The practice took hold in Japan because there is so little land to spare for either use alone.

Tunisia  تونس

Shade as a resource

The Tunisian oasis already farms in layers: palms on top, fruit trees beneath, vegetables at ground level. The top layer exists to cut the sun. A raised panel is a new version of a very old answer — one that can also run the pump.

What Gaia builds on it

Four energy modules.

Solar Sharing Advisor

Planned

Works out panel height, spacing and orientation so the shade helps the crop instead of starving it.

The most original idea in this chapter, and the one that needs real light data per crop.

Solar Farming AI

Planned

Estimates what a parcel could produce in electricity alongside its harvest, and what that changes for the farm.

Needs irradiance data and local tariffs. Designed, not built.

Smart Pumping

Planned

Moves pumping to the hours when the panels produce, so water moves on sunlight.

The simplest to build first: a pump controller and a clock.

Energy Prediction

Planned

A short-range forecast of what the installation will produce, so irrigation can be planned around it.

Shares the weather feed with chapter 二.

Nothing in this chapter is measured. No installation exists yet. Gaia describes what the combination would do, not what it has done.

Working Demo Planned

Every feature carries one of these. Working runs for real; Demo works on sample values; Planned is designed but not built.
