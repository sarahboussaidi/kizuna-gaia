Prologue  ·  The meaning of kizuna

One word for what holds two things together.

絆 — kizuna — is written with the radical for thread, 糸. It first meant a tether: the rope that kept an animal tied to its place. Over time it came to mean something gentler — the bonds between people that are hard to cut.

Gaia is built on that second meaning. Japan and Tunisia farm different crops, on different soils, under different skies. They share one question: how to keep land fertile, water well used and energy clean for whoever farms it next.

Two landscapes, one challenge

Different skies. The same hillside problem.

A terraced valley in rural Japan and a dry slope in southern Tunisia look nothing alike. Both are trying to keep water, soil and a living on a piece of ground that would rather let them go.

四

How Gaia thinks

It does not copy Japan into Tunisia. It checks what survives the journey.

一

You describe your land

Slope, soil, rainfall, water source, power, crop — the way you would describe it to a neighbour.

二

Gaia tests each practice

Every Japanese or Tunisian technique lists what it needs. Gaia rules out the ones your parcel cannot support, and says why.

三

You get three actions

Not a report: the practice that fits, how well it fits, and three things to do in the field this month.
